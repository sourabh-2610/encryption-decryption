What is Encryption & Decryption?

The two sides of the same coin that keep your data safe

🔐

Encryption

The process of converting plaintext (readable data) into ciphertext (scrambled, unreadable data) using a mathematical algorithm and a secret key. Think of it like locking your diary with a key — only someone with the same key can read it.

🔓

Decryption

The reverse of encryption — transforming ciphertext back into plaintext. It requires the correct key to unlock the scrambled data. Without the right key, the data remains completely unreadable — like gibberish.

  • 1

    You write your message (Plaintext)

    This is the original readable data — like typing "My password is 1234" in a message.

  • 2

    A secret key is used

    The key is like a password that tells the algorithm how to scramble the data. Without this key, nobody can reverse the process.

  • 3

    The algorithm scrambles the data

    The encryption algorithm (like AES-256) takes your plaintext + key and runs complex math to produce completely unreadable output.

  • 4

    Output is Ciphertext

    The result is scrambled, random-looking text like "aX9#kL2@mP..." — even if someone intercepts it, they can't read it.

  • 2

    You need the exact same key that was used for encryption. Even one character difference and it won't work!

  • 4

    The readable message is restored — "My password is 1234" — exactly as it was before encryption.

Everyday Life

Simple Real-Life Examples

You already use encryption every day — here's how it works in simple terms

💬

Sending a WhatsApp Message

When you type "Happy Birthday!" to your friend, WhatsApp encrypts it before sending. Even if a hacker intercepts the message in transit, they only see gibberish. Your friend's phone has the key to decrypt it back to "Happy Birthday!"

🏠 Analogy: Like putting your letter in a locked box — only your friend has the key to open it.
💳

Making an Online Payment

When you enter your card number on Amazon, your browser encrypts it using HTTPS. The scrambled card number travels through the internet safely. Only Amazon's server can decrypt and read your actual card number.

🔐 Analogy: Like speaking in a secret code over the phone — eavesdroppers hear nonsense.
📱

Your Phone's Lock Screen

When you set a PIN or password on your phone, all your photos, messages, and apps are encrypted. If someone steals your phone, they can't access anything — the data is scrambled without your PIN to decrypt it.

🏦 Analogy: Like a bank vault — even if thieves break in, they can't open the vault without the combination.
